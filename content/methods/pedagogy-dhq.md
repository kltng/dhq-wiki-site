# Pedagogy in DHQ

## Overview

Pedagogy appears as a keyword in 97 DHQ articles, representing the journal's sustained attention to how digital humanities is taught and learned. DHQ's pedagogy coverage spans undergraduate and graduate education, digital literacy, the design of learning environments, and the integration of computational methods into humanities curricula. The journal documents a field that is not only researching digital methods but actively building the educational infrastructure to transmit them to new generations of scholars.

A distinctive feature of DHQ's pedagogy coverage is its emphasis on *project-based learning* — the idea that students learn DH best by doing DH, by participating in real digital projects that produce real scholarly outputs. This approach blurs the boundary between teaching and research, making pedagogy a form of knowledge production rather than mere transmission.

## Arguments and Evolution

### Learning by Doing

Christopher Blackwell and Thomas Martin (DHQ:000024) make a compelling case for involving undergraduate students in genuine DH research projects, arguing that this experience teaches both technical skills and humanistic habits of mind more effectively than traditional classroom instruction. Their work with the Homer Multitext project demonstrates how students can make real scholarly contributions while learning.

### Digital Literacy as Humanities Competency

Several DHQ articles argue that digital literacy is not a separate skill set but an integral part of contemporary humanities education. Jeff Howard (DHQ:000002) connects game-based pedagogy with interpretative theory, while Craig Bellamy (DHQ:000119) examines how virtual research environments can serve as pedagogical tools that teach both technical methods and critical digital literacy.

### Institutional Challenges

DHQ pedagogy articles also address the institutional challenges of DH education: how to build cross-listed courses, how to staff interdisciplinary programs, how to evaluate digital student work, and how to secure institutional support for DH pedagogical innovation. Lynne Siemens's work (DHQ:000114) on developing academic capacity in DH includes a strong pedagogical dimension.

### Global and Community Pedagogy

Amy Earhart and Maura Ives's introduction to their special issue (DHQ:000050), along with Toton and Martin's work (DHQ:000047), reflect DHQ's attention to how DH pedagogy can serve broader community engagement and public humanities goals — bringing DH methods beyond the university to engage with communities, cultural institutions, and public audiences.
