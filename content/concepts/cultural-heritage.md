# Cultural Heritage and Area Studies in DHQ

## Overview

The combination of cultural heritage (57 articles) and area studies (64 articles) represents approximately 121 articles addressing the intersection of digital methods with the preservation, interpretation, and dissemination of cultural materials from specific geographic, linguistic, and cultural traditions. DHQ's coverage spans classical antiquity, medieval manuscripts, non-Western textual traditions, indigenous knowledge systems, and the digital infrastructure needed to support cross-cultural research.

A distinctive feature of this coverage is its attention to the *specificity* of cultural materials — the ways in which different textual traditions, writing systems, and cultural practices demand different digital approaches. DHQ articles on Tibetan endangered texts (DHQ:000004), Latin incunabula (DHQ:000027), and Black Drama (DHQ:000043) demonstrate that digital methods cannot be applied uniformly across cultural contexts but must be adapted to local conditions.

## Arguments and Evolution

### Endangered Knowledge and Digital Preservation

Linda Patrik's article on Tibetan texts (DHQ:000004) exemplifies DHQ's concern with using digital methods to preserve endangered knowledge systems. The article highlights the particular challenges of encoding non-Western scripts and the ethical dimensions of making sensitive cultural materials digitally accessible.

### Classics as Digital Frontier

A remarkable cluster of articles (DHQ:000023–000035) from a special issue on classics and cyberinfrastructure demonstrates how classical philology has been a testing ground for large-scale digital scholarship. Gregory Crane and colleagues argue that the comprehensive digitization of classical texts — combined with NLP, machine learning, and linked data — has the potential to transform not just classical studies but the humanities more broadly, by making the full textual record of a civilization computationally accessible.

### Crowdsourcing and Community Engagement

Tim Causer and Valerie Wallace's account of the Transcribe Bentham project (DHQ:000125) demonstrates how cultural heritage digitization can engage broader publics through crowdsourcing, creating new forms of scholarly community and public scholarship.

### Area Studies and Global DH

DHQ's area studies coverage includes articles on French literature, Black Drama, Latin American digital publishing, and various non-Anglophone traditions. This coverage reflects the journal's commitment to a global vision of digital humanities that extends beyond the Anglophone center.
